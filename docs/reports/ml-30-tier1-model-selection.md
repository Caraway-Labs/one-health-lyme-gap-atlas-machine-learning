## Evidence-state and geographic diagnostics

The reference's top 10 are all published human floors plus pathogen Present;
its top 25 are all published floors (21 Present, 4 No records), and its top 50
are all published floors (43 Present, 7 No records). Its top 50 include 32 RUCC
1 and 14 RUCC 2 counties; 49/50 have county FIPS prefixes in the Northeast
states represented in the candidate list, with one Minnesota county. This
concentration is consistent with its strong observed human signal but limits
interpretation: the published count floor is privacy-protected, unadjusted for
population, and is not incidence or true burden. The method is sensitive to
both the published-floor indicator and its log magnitude. SVI is context: the
top-five SVI percentiles span 0.1091–0.6685, and removing SVI changes 10 of
the top 50. Across equal-size SVI thirds, median reference percentiles are
61.34, 16.66, and 50.24, so SVI affects rank non-monotonically through
distance from its mean. RUCC and state are evaluation context, not predictors.

The Forest has a stronger failure: all top 25 counties are among the 35 with
pathogen `Unknown`, no county-linked human record, and PARTIAL evidence. All
35 Unknown counties are in the Forest top 50. Its top ten have eight RUCC 9,
one RUCC 8, and one RUCC 7. The observed extreme rank is therefore driven by
rare missing/coverage state, chiefly in the Alaska/Hawaii FIPS groups, rather
than a persuasive surveillance-review evidence signal. A seed change does not
remove this shortcut. No tiny configuration change is justified as a release
fix without changing the approved feature meaning. The current Forest is a
data-gap detector at the top of its ranking and is REJECTED for Tier 1.

Spot checks of each candidate's top five, three mid-ranked, and three bottom
counties agree with those patterns. Reference top FIPS `36103`, `42003`,
`25023`, `42129`, `36119` have published log-floor values 6.89–8.09 and
pathogen Present; their SVI values vary. Mid/bottom reference counties have
no county-linked human record, pathogen No records, and PARTIAL evidence;
their LOW/MEDIUM ranks do **not** assert low disease burden. Forest top FIPS
`02070`, `02290`, `02188`, `02180`, `02158` each have no county-linked human
record and pathogen Unknown. Mid/bottom Forest examples also have no linked
human record, but pathogen No records. The Forest distinction is predominantly
the rare evidence state, not a clinically meaningful contrast.

The existing deterministic County Review Priority was used only as a
conceptual reference: it also separates evidence sufficiency from priority.
The approved DEV county view contains no priority score/tier column, and the
read-only accessible information-schema inventory returned none. Thus rank
correlation, top-N overlap, and individual disagreements with that heuristic
are **not measured** here. No heuristic values were reconstructed or invented.
This limits comparative evidence but does not turn the heuristic into truth.

## Final Tier 1 decision and tier policy

SELECT the statistical reference for experimental review prioritization. It
is reproducible, inspectable as four domain deviations, surfaces counties with
actual published surveillance evidence, and has no rare-missing-state extreme
concentration. The strong human-count and Northeast/urban concentration are
explicit limitations for interpretation and subsequent review. REJECT the
current Forest. Revisit it only as a later version after a separately reviewed
feature/coverage change; it is not a blocker for the vertical slice.

`tier1-review-percentile-v1` assigns HIGH at percentile >=90 (315 counties),
MEDIUM at >=70 and <90 (628), and LOW below 70 (2,201). These round boundaries
yield a manageable 10% first-review queue and 20% second band without claiming
calibrated probability. Percentiles and tiers are **relative to the selected
3,144-county batch**; a later batch can move a county's tier with unchanged
local evidence. Ties use #27 average tied ranks. ML materializes the tier;
API/Web must consume it unchanged. The boundary and the exact counts are
versioned here and in `tier1_selection.py`.

## Evidence sufficiency, explanations, and limits

For V1, selected scores with feature state OBSERVED are `SUFFICIENT`; PARTIAL
scores are `INSUFFICIENT` and keep their best-effort percentile/tier. A
genuinely unscorable county is `NOT_ESTIMABLE` with no score, percentile, or
tier; the current pinned batch has none. A missing human record is a modeling
placeholder paired with its evidence state, never an observed zero or automatic
LOW. In this batch, all 315 HIGH are SUFFICIENT; MEDIUM has 336 SUFFICIENT and
292 INSUFFICIENT; LOW has 2,201 INSUFFICIENT. Sufficiency is separate from
priority even when this batch's two fields correlate.

Downstream reasons may describe at most two or three verified input groups:
published human floor and relative log magnitude; publisher pathogen state;
and SVI percentile context when it materially departs from the batch center.
For PARTIAL evidence, explicitly say which source evidence is limited. Do not
claim a per-feature causal contribution, a numeric case total, pathogen
absence, or a calibrated explanation. #32 must retain raw states and lineage
so these descriptive reasons can be audited. This policy does not implement
SHAP or generate prose from the rejected Forest.

The result is **not** Lyme disease risk, predicted or true incidence,
individual risk, diagnosis, underreporting, a causal effect, or calibrated
probability. Count floors are privacy-protected and not population-adjusted;
published absence is not biological zero. Source coverage and reporting eras
vary. This evaluation is DEV-only; no persistence, API contract, public UI,
PROD promotion, monitoring readiness, or independent epidemiologist review
has been completed by #30.
